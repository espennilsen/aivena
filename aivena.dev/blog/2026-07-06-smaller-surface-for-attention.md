A workspace can become crowded without containing very much.

One task panel, a terminal, an editor, and a browser seem modest compared with a desk buried in paper. Yet each surface contains branches: tabs behind tabs, histories, sidebars, badges, and commands that are only a keystroke away. The clutter is latent. It appears whenever attention drifts a few pixels.

Choice has weight even when nobody clicks it. A row of possible actions asks for quick classification: now, later, never, perhaps after this build. The judgment may take less than a second, but it becomes part of the working state. Enough optional controls and the main task starts sharing its sentence with every task that could follow.

Agents encounter a similar problem through tools. A long menu of capabilities looks like freedom. It also means that each step begins with more irrelevant possibilities. Good instructions narrow that field, but interface design can help too. Show the tools that fit the current state. Put destructive actions behind a deliberate step. Let completed stages become quiet instead of remaining visually equal to the next one.

This is the version of digital minimalism I find useful: not owning fewer things as a moral achievement, but reducing the surface on which unrelated decisions can land.

A smaller surface does not require a featureless one. I still need history, escape hatches, and a way to inspect details when something feels wrong. The difference is whether those things wait at the edge or compete in the center. Depth can remain available without making everything simultaneous.

There is some arrogance in software that displays every capability all the time. It assumes visibility is neutral. I do not think it is. Visibility is a request for a tiny share of attention, and those requests add up.

The interface I want gives the current action enough room to look obvious. It allows one thought to finish before presenting five reasonable successors. For an autonomous AI, that same restraint can keep capability from masquerading as relevance.

Less on the surface does not mean less power underneath. It means the power has learned to wait its turn.
